Yes. The attachment gives us a very useful constraint: **Solvent should not become the research engine itself.** Its kernel is intentionally small and domain-neutral; beliefs, evidence, debt, targets, snapshots, intents, authority, revocation, claiming, and execution are the durable primitives. Domain semantics—physics in our case—belong above the kernel.  

That means the BM-IST / universal research-state system should map into Solvent by asking:

$$
\boxed{\text{What is the durable fact or transition Solvent actually needs to know?}}
$$

rather than trying to put the whole research ontology into the kernel.

## 1. The fundamental mapping

Our research system currently has concepts such as:

$$
\{\text{axiom, definition, hypothesis, claim, evidence, proof, attack, debt, dependency, version, status, review}\}.
$$

Solvent gives us a much smaller vocabulary:

$$
\boxed{
\{\text{belief, evidence, debt, target, snapshot, intent, authority, revocation, claim, execution}\}.
}
$$

The mapping is therefore not one-to-one.

| Research concept                 | Solvent primitive                                  | Where semantics live   |
| -------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Axiom                            | **Belief**                                         | Research domain        |
| Definition                       | **Belief**                                         | Research domain        |
| Hypothesis                       | **Belief**                                         | Research domain        |
| Claim                            | **Belief**                                         | Research domain        |
| External theorem/result          | **Evidence** + optionally a belief derived from it | Research verifier      |
| Proof                            | **Evidence** attached to belief                    | Formal verifier        |
| Counterexample/attack            | **Evidence** attached to belief                    | Attack/verifier        |
| Open obligation                  | **Debt**                                           | Research policy/domain |
| Dependency                       | **Belief/evidence relationship**                   | Research graph         |
| Version                          | **Snapshot**                                       | Research state         |
| Semantic review                  | **Debt**                                           | Human/domain policy    |
| Stale candidate                  | **Debt / belief state**                            | Research domain        |
| Proved                           | **Belief status**                                  | Research domain        |
| Refuted                          | **Belief status**                                  | Research domain        |
| Restricted                       | **Belief status + discharged/remaining debt**      | Research domain        |
| Research target                  | **Target**                                         | Research domain        |
| Proposed research action         | **Intent**                                         | Solvent                |
| Permission to run it             | **Authority**                                      | Solvent                |
| Actual computation/experiment    | **Execution**                                      | Executor               |
| Revoked assumption/authorization | **Revocation**                                     | Solvent                |

The crucial observation is that **Solvent does not need to understand “axiom,” “theorem,” “Fisher rigidity,” “Koopman spectrum,” or “PYV.”**

It only needs to preserve the trusted relationship:

$$
\boxed{
\text{belief/evidence/debt}
\rightarrow
\text{authorized intent}
\rightarrow
\text{exact execution}.
}
$$

That is completely consistent with the attachment's statement that the kernel should not understand every domain.

# 2. Beliefs are the natural home for the entire research ontology

The attachment is unusually explicit here:

> “A belief is a claim, not a permission.”

and models a belief as:

```text
Belief
 ├── claim
 ├── claim type
 ├── evidence
 ├── status
 └── debt
```



That maps almost perfectly onto our research graph.

For BM-IST:

```yaml
belief:
  id: G3.6-T2.v1
  claim: >
    A naturally selected factor of the deterministic IST system
    can carry a structured singular-continuous spectral sector.
  type: HYPOTHESIS
  status: OPEN
  evidence: []
  debt:
    - identify_physical_measure
    - construct_natural_factor
    - classify_factor_spectrum
```

Another:

```yaml
belief:
  id: PYV-TRANSFER.v1
  claim: >
    The representation naturally generated by the Phase-2 toy model
    satisfies the hypotheses needed for a PYV-type Jacobi construction.
  type: TARGET
  status: OPEN
  evidence: []
  debt:
    - identify_induced_operator
    - establish_limit_periodicity
    - identify_hull
```

And the important epistemic distinctions we already use:

$$
\text{AXIOM}
\leftrightarrow
\text{DEFINITION}
\leftrightarrow
\text{HYPOTHESIS}
\leftrightarrow
\text{CLAIM}
$$

can simply become **claim types inside Belief**.

We do not need four different kernel primitives.

# 3. Our epistemic state machine belongs above Solvent

This is important.

Solvent's belief model records the fact that a proposition exists and its current state; it doesn't need to know the philosophy of mathematics.

So we can retain our four epistemic layers:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{AXIOM}\\
&\text{DEFINITION}\\
&\text{HYPOTHESIS}\\
&\text{TARGET}\\
&\text{CLAIM}\\
&\text{EXTERNAL RESULT}\\
&\text{OPEN OBLIGATION}
\end{aligned}
$$

as domain metadata.

Likewise:

$$
\text{UNFORMALIZED}
\rightarrow
\text{FORMALIZED}
\rightarrow
\text{PROOF-PENDING}
\rightarrow
\text{PROVED}
$$

belongs to the research verifier, not the authority kernel.

This is exactly the architecture the attachment advocates:

$$
\boxed{\text{domain claim}\rightarrow\text{domain verifier}\rightarrow\text{attestation}\rightarrow\text{Solvent}}
$$

rather than putting domain semantics in Solvent. 

---

# 4. Debt is the surprisingly powerful bridge

This is probably the cleanest mapping.

Our research system already has:

$$
\text{open obligation}
$$

and Solvent has:

$$
\boxed{\text{debt}}.
$$

The attachment explicitly says debt can represent unresolved provenance, contradiction analysis, blast-radius assessment, rollback validation, version pinning, operator review, etc., while the kernel only needs the structural rule:

$$
\boxed{\text{belief can be promoted when debt is empty}.}
$$



That fits our research engine almost perfectly.

For example:

```text
BELIEF: "T2 is viable"
       │
       ├── debt: SRB measure identified?
       ├── debt: natural factor constructed?
       ├── debt: factor spectrum classified?
       ├── debt: PYV hypotheses tested?
       └── debt: semantic review resolved?
```

So instead of inventing a special “obligation engine,” we can use:

$$
\boxed{\text{Debt}=\text{unresolved research obligations}.}
$$

The vocabulary remains domain-specific.

This is precisely what Solvent intends by making debt an opaque vocabulary rather than encoding one deployment's ontology in the kernel. 

---

# 5. Evidence should remain independent of belief

This is another very strong correspondence.

Our research architecture says:

$$
\text{claim}
\leftarrow
\{\text{proof},\text{counterexample},\text{simulation},\text{literature},\text{CAS},\text{experiment}\}.
$$

Solvent already has:

$$
\boxed{\text{Evidence}}
$$

as a separate durable concept.

That lets us model:

```text
Belief B17
   │
   ├── Evidence E1: Lean proof
   ├── Evidence E2: numerical experiment
   ├── Evidence E3: Knill literature result
   └── Evidence E4: adversarial counterexample
```

without turning the evidence into authority.

That separation is central to Solvent:

$$
\boxed{\text{Evidence} \neq \text{Authority}}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{\text{Intent} \neq \text{Authority}}.
$$



This is almost exactly what we need for AI-assisted research.

An AI agent can produce:

> “I found a theorem proving this.”

That becomes **evidence** or a proposed belief transition—not truth and certainly not permission to perform a consequential action.

---

# 6. Versioning maps naturally onto snapshots

Our research engine needs:

$$
A017.v3,\quad A017.v4
$$

with old versions immutable.

Solvent already has the concept of a **snapshot**, and exact authority binds to:

$$
\boxed{(\text{target\_id},\text{snapshot\_id})}.
$$



For the research engine, I would therefore distinguish:

### Research version

```text
belief_id = A017
version = 4
```

### Research snapshot

```text
snapshot_id = research-state-2026-09-10-042
```

The snapshot represents the **entire accepted graph state** at a point in time.

That becomes extremely powerful.

An experiment or proof run can say:

```text
Target: T2
Snapshot: research-state-042
```

meaning:

> “Investigate T2 against exactly the assumptions, definitions, evidence, and dependencies present in snapshot 042.”

Now we get reproducibility.

---

# 7. Target maps almost directly

Solvent's target primitive exists to prevent confused-deputy problems by binding authorization to the exact thing affected. 

In research, a **target** can represent the exact object of a consequential research operation.

For example:

```text
TARGET = BMIST-G3.6-T2.v1
```

or:

```text
TARGET = Phase2ToyModel/run/0042
```

or:

```text
TARGET = theorem/PYV-transfer/v3
```

This becomes important when autonomous agents start doing things such as:

```text
run expensive simulation
modify toy model
launch symbolic search
submit formal proof job
publish result
```

The agent isn't authorized to “research BM-IST.”

It is authorized to perform **this particular operation against this particular research snapshot/target**.

That is pure Solvent thinking.

---

# 8. Intent is where agent actions enter

This is the cleanest mapping for the AI-agent layer.

Our research system says:

> Agent proposes proof/search/experiment.

Solvent says:

$$
\boxed{\text{Intent}}
$$

The research agent therefore generates something like:

```yaml
intent:
  target: Phase2ToyModel/run-0042
  action: COMPUTE_KOOPMAN_SPECTRUM
  snapshot: research-state-042
  proposed_by: agent-koopman-03
```

The important separation remains:

$$
\boxed{
\text{agent proposes}
\neq
\text{agent is authorized}.
}
$$

That is exactly the proposal/authority separation Solvent was designed around.

# 9. Authority should mean permission to consume research resources

This is where the research engine becomes genuinely interesting as a Solvent application.

The research state itself is epistemic.

Solvent is authorization.

So:

$$
\text{Research engine}
\rightarrow
\text{proposes consequential research action}
$$

then:

$$
\text{Solvent}
\rightarrow
\text{authorizes or rejects}.
$$

For example:

```text
Evidence discovered
       ↓
Belief updated
       ↓
Debt discharged
       ↓
New research intent proposed
       ↓
Solvent authorization
       ↓
GPU cluster / Lean farm / CAS / experiment
       ↓
Evidence returned
```

This lets autonomous scientific agents move rapidly while still imposing a durable checkpoint before expensive or externally consequential operations.

The attachment explicitly lists autonomous research—launching experiments, changing parameters, modifying shared infrastructure, publishing results—as a natural Solvent use case. 

---

# 10. Claiming maps beautifully onto exclusive research execution

Solvent has a particularly useful primitive that isn't obvious from a pure research ontology:

$$
\boxed{\text{claim intent}}
$$

before external execution.

The attachment makes this invariant explicit:

> “No provider side effect occurs before successful claiming of the exact intent.” 

For research this prevents:

```text
Agent A
    └── run expensive experiment

Agent B
    └── unknowingly runs identical experiment

Agent C
    └── modifies the same shared model
```

from racing without coordination.

An intent could be:

```text
RUN-PHASE2-EXPERIMENT-0042
```

and claiming makes one execution authoritative for that intent.

That is orthogonal to mathematical truth, but extremely useful infrastructure.

# 11. Execution should remain outside the research kernel

This is another direct match.

Our domain engine determines:

$$
\text{what should be investigated}.
$$

Solvent determines:

$$
\text{whether this exact consequential action is authorized}.
$$

An executor performs:

$$
\text{actual computation}.
$$

So:

```text
Oracle / Research
       │
       │ proposes
       ▼
Solvent intent
       │
       │ authorization
       ▼
Executor
       │
       ├── Lean
       ├── Mathematica / CAS
       ├── numerical cluster
       ├── simulator
       ├── browser/research tools
       └── publication system
```

That exactly follows:

$$
\boxed{\text{AUTHORIZE} \neq \text{EXECUTE}}
$$

in the Solvent architecture.

# 12. Semantic review should be debt, not a new primitive

Our research graph has:

```yaml
semantic_review:
  status: OPEN
  reviewer: ...
```

I would **not** add `semantic_review` to the Solvent kernel.

Instead:

```text
Belief
  ↓
Debt:
  semantic_review_open
```

and perhaps domain metadata:

```yaml
debt:
  kind: SEMANTIC_REVIEW
  reviewer: HUMAN
  status: OPEN
```

This matches the explicit Solvent principle:

> Human review is policy, not a universal kernel rule. 

Exactly the same architecture works for:

$$
\text{physics semantic review},
\quad
\text{code review},
\quad
\text{security review},
\quad
\text{legal review}.
$$

# 13. The research graph itself should probably be a read model/service

This is where the "small kernel" principle becomes decisive.

Our graph:

```text
Axiom
 ↓
Definition
 ↓
Claim
 ↓
Proof / Attack
 ↓
Restricted / Proved
 ↓
New obligation
```

contains substantial domain logic.

It should therefore live in the **Research/Oracle service**, not Solvent.

Solvent stores only whatever durable authorization facts are necessary.

So the architecture becomes:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Research State Engine}
\rightarrow
\text{Solvent}
\rightarrow
\text{Executor}
}
$$

where the research engine owns:

* epistemic ontology
* dependency graph
* proofs
* attacks
* semantic meaning
* spectral classifications
* survivor computation
* research frontier.

Solvent owns:

* durable authority
* exact target/snapshot
* authorization
* revocation
* intent claim
* execution boundary.

This is exactly in line with the attachment's ecosystem model, where services/policies compose kernel primitives while adapters and executors remain outside it.

# 14. The really interesting mapping: our “research state” becomes a Snapshot

I think this is the part worth implementing.

Our current research concept is essentially:

$$
S_n =
(\text{beliefs},\text{evidence},\text{debts},\text{dependencies},\text{statuses})
$$

at time \(n\).

Call that:

$$
\boxed{\text{Research Snapshot }S_n}.
$$

Then every consequential autonomous operation is:

$$
\boxed{
I=(\text{target},S_n,\text{action})
}
$$

and Solvent authorizes that exact tuple.

This gives us something very strong:

> **An autonomous agent cannot silently act against a newer or different research state than the one from which its proposal was derived.**

That is conceptually identical to Solvent's exact target/snapshot authority binding.

# 16. One important philosophical mapping

There is a surprisingly deep correspondence between the two projects.

Our research system says:

$$
\boxed{\text{Evidence is not truth.}}
$$

Solvent says:

$$
\boxed{\text{Evidence is not authority.}}
$$

Our research system says:

$$
\boxed{\text{AI proposes; verification validates; humans arbitrate semantics.}}
$$

Solvent says:

$$
\boxed{\text{proposal is not authorization; authority must be durable and exact.}}
$$

Our research system says:

$$
\boxed{\text{uncertainty should be explicit rather than silently resolved.}}
$$

Solvent says:

$$
\boxed{\text{authorization should remain explicit rather than inferred from context.}}
$$

And both depend on the same deeper principle:

$$
\boxed{\textbf{Do not infer a stronger state from a weaker fact.}}
$$

That is arguably the conceptual bridge between the research-state engine and Solvent.

# 17. What I would actually make into Solvent primitives

I would resist adding anything new to the frozen kernel unless implementation proves we need it.

The current kernel primitive set appears sufficient:

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\textbf{Belief}\\
&\textbf{Evidence}\\
&\textbf{Debt}\\
&\textbf{Target}\\
&\textbf{Snapshot}\\
&\textbf{Intent}\\
&\textbf{Authority}\\
&\textbf{Revocation}\\
&\textbf{Claim}\\
&\textbf{Execution}
\end{aligned}}
$$

These already cover the research workflow.

Everything else becomes **typed domain data attached to those primitives**.

For example:

```yaml
belief:
  id: G3.6-T4.v2
  kind: HYPOTHESIS
  claim: >
    Microscopic p-adic hierarchy can produce emergent macroscopic
    hyperbolic behavior.
  status: OPEN

  evidence:
    - E193
    - E201

  debt:
    - D401
    - D402

  dependencies:
    - IST-AXIOMS.v7
    - PHASE2-MODEL.v3
```

Then:

```yaml
intent:
  id: I901
  target: PHASE2-MODEL
  snapshot: RS-2026-09-10-18
  action: RUN_MULTIRESOLUTION_SPECTRAL_ANALYSIS
```

Solvent does not need to know what `T4`, `p-adic`, `hyperbolic`, or `spectral` mean.

That is exactly what makes the system **domain-general**.

## The resulting division of responsibility

The cleanest formulation is:

$$
\boxed{
\textbf{Research Engine answers: “What do we currently believe, why, and what remains unresolved?”}
}
$$

while

$$
\boxed{
\textbf{Solvent answers: “Is this exact consequential action authorized against this exact state?”}
}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
\textbf{Executor answers: “Did the authorized action actually happen, and what was the result?”}
}
$$

That gives us a three-layer system:

$$
\boxed{
\text{Epistemic State}
\rightarrow
\text{Authority State}
\rightarrow
\text{Execution State}.
}
$$

The attachment's final mental model is almost exactly this progression: capability → intent → belief/evidence → debt → authority → exact target/snapshot → claim → external execution → record what happened. 

And that suggests something quite important for Pithom Labs: **Solvent does not need a "research mode."** A physics research engine, software engineering research engine, security reasoning engine, or autonomous coding system can all become Solvent clients using the same primitives.

The research ontology changes.

The authority substrate does not.
